REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE: GAP MATRIX

Project FALCON: An Affordable Solar-Powered Smart Coastal Monitoring Buoy with Local Edge Processing and AI-Assisted Short-Term Wave-Height Prediction
Project FALCON Research Group · Fullbright College · August 2026

Purpose. This matrix compares related research, identifies what remains unresolved, and shows the specific contribution that Project FALCON can test. The gap column does not mean that prior studies are defective; it identifies differences in scope, environment, hardware, validation, or intended users.

Demonstrated that low-cost GNSS can capture useful coastal sea-level and wave information.		The deployment was short and used post-processed GNSS in a different coastline. Presented orientation correction, acceleration transformation, integration, and spectral processing for a compact inertial wave sensor.		The work validates the measurement principle, not the BNO085 or FALCON hull. Used signal decomposition and deep learning for nonlinear, non-stationary wave prediction. Identified drift, corrosion, power limits, communication problems, and missing references as persistent data-quality risks.		The paper provides broad quality strategies rather than a complete low-cost coastal implementation. FALCON can operationalize them through per-record quality flags, missing/stuck-value checks, cross-sensor plausibility, local buffering, maintenance logs, and prediction suppression when inputs are invalid.Skålvik et al. Monitoring Tides, Currents, and Waves along Coastal Habitats Using the Mini Buoy
Validated reinforced low-cost accelerometer-based devices for inundation, currents, and wave orbital velocity.		The bottom-mounted Mini Buoy has a different mechanics and purpose from a surface-following, solar-powered telemetry buoy. Interpretation: Existing literature supports the feasibility of each major subsystem, but no reviewed study validates the same complete combination, cost constraints, local-first architecture, and intended Palawan coastal use case proposed for FALCON.

Consolidated Research Gap

Prior studies establish that low-cost buoys, inertial or pressure-assisted wave measurement, GNSS monitoring, renewable power, and machine-learning wave prediction are individually feasible. However, the reviewed literature does not establish that these capabilities will remain accurate and dependable when integrated into a student-built, solar-powered buoy using inexpensive sensors, intermittent connectivity, and a small edge computer in a Philippine near-shore environment. In particular, there is limited evidence on the combined effects of hull and mooring response, sensor quality, local data scarcity, power constraints, model uncertainty, offline operation, and false displacement alarms within one reproducible low-cost platform.

Proposed Novel Contribution of Project FALCON

  1. Locally adapted integration: A reproducible ESP32–Orange Pi coastal buoy designed around affordable, obtainable components and a defined Palawan test site.
  2. Calibrated wave observation: Wave estimates derived from synchronized motion and pressure measurements, with hull/mooring effects and reference comparison explicitly documented.
  3. Honest edge prediction: A clearly defined 5–15 minute wave-height target evaluated chronologically against persistence and other simple baselines, including error, latency, and uncertainty.
  4. Local-first resilience: Onboard acquisition, quality checks, storage, and dashboard service that continue during internet outages and recover delayed records without silently duplicating data.
  5. Integrated health and displacement logic: GPS, IMU, time persistence, sensor health, and power state combined to distinguish likely abnormal movement from ordinary anchor swing and GPS noise.
  6. Whole-system validation: Measurement accuracy, energy autonomy, communication reliability, maintainability, and alert performance assessed together rather than presenting component integration alone as novelty.

Defensible novelty statement: Project FALCON is novel not because it invents the smart buoy, wave sensor, or machine-learning forecast, but because it designs and experimentally evaluates their affordable, local-first integration for a defined Philippine coastal setting, with transparent data quality, uncertainty, energy performance, and displacement-alert limitations.
