When to use Option B

  • Backup choose this only if stability becomes the top concern wide stance, easier deck access
  • Two pontoon floats + deck more stable but more fasteners, joints, and transport width
  • Panel roof above cabinet clean layout, but final wattage must follow measured load
  • Pressure sensor hangs inside a small guard cage between hulls easy to service, protected by the pontoon spacing
  • Visible bridle-to-anchor study shows required chain/rope/anchor sizing before field validation
  • Trade-off: likely higher fabrication effort than Option A

Buying note

  • Existing ESP32 kit, breadboard, jumper wires, and buck converters may be reused for bench validation, but replacement prices remain in the proposal budget.
  • Water pressure sensor must be confirmed first: low range, 4–20 mA output, IP68, cable type, and seawater/material compatibility.
  • Use Wi-Fi first for lab or near-shore tests; buy LTE only if the validation site has no reliable Wi-Fi.
  • Final battery and solar size depends on measured current draw from ESP32, GPS, wind, pressure interface, internet module, and flashing beacon.
